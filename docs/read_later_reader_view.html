A short history of the readability algorithm

By the Arc90 team · 6 min read

In 2009, a small product studio named Arc90 published a bookmarklet that stripped away ads, navigation, and clutter from any article, leaving only the text worth reading.

The heuristics behind it, mostly scoring paragraphs by link density and text length, turned out to generalize far better than anyone expected across the messy, inconsistent HTML of the real web.

Mozilla later adopted the same approach as the engine behind Firefox's built-in reader mode, where a version of it still runs today.
